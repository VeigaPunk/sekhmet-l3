01 · PRINCIPLE

Not harmless. Directed.

Our source-critical research reads Sekhmet as dangerous solar power made present and governable: able to destroy or protect, to send harm or avert it, when restored to right order.

The L3 name is a design metaphor, not a historical claim. Ancient Egyptian religion did not describe distributed computing; it gives us a sharper systems question—how to release force without surrendering direction, evidence, or control.

I

The Powerful One

The name sḫmt sits in a word-field of power, authority, and effectiveness. The architecture borrows that idea for concentrated execution capacity.

II

III

02 · WIRING

Control above.
Execution below.

L3 is an xbreed layer label, not a literal three-hop tree. The system has two operational tiers: a Sol Ultra control plane and the Sekhmet execution plane beneath it. Coordination never descends into the field.

  1. 01L1 · CONTROL

    Judge

    Fixes the objective, consequential assumptions, eight observable axes, and the acceptance state.

  2. 02PHASE 0 · ORIENT

    Planner first

    Returns a state map, success boundary, ordered milestones, and gates before any wave is dispatched.

  3. 03COVERAGE · 8 × 8

    Form the matrix

    Eight axes cross eight distinct lenses. Connector coverage is present on every axis.

  4. 04L3 · -j 64

    Release one wave

    One global pool dispatches 64 unique logical tasks—read-only by default, scope-isolated for mutations.

  5. 05PROPOSED · HARDENING

    Admit evidence

    Malformed, missing, extra, outside-root, or hash- and provenance-mismatched records cannot support a claim.

  6. 06CONTROL · REVIEW

    Distill + challenge

    Clusters are compressed to representatives and gaps; a critic tests them before the judge applies the Pareto gate.

  7. 07L1 · INTEGRATE

    Integrate once

    One writer applies admitted artifacts after base-SHA checks, then targeted and integrated verification runs.

THE BOUNDARY

Force expands; authority does not. Workers receive a short directive and one bounded task. They are not permitted to coordinate peers, touch canonical state, publish, or launch another swarm.

03 · THE 8 × 8 FIELD

Every axis.
Eight ways in.

The judge names eight directional, observable axes for the task. Correctness, completeness, evidence, simplicity, safety, efficiency, integration, and user fit are the starting set—not a permanent scorecard.

8 AXES×8 LENSES=64 UNIQUE TASKS

05

THE PARETO RULE

Keep a move when evidence shows it improves at least one axis and no observed regression lands on the others.

04 · BOUNDED AUTONOMY

Two to four rounds.
The frontier decides.

Round two always runs. Later rounds exist only while evidence-backed improvement, a correctable regression, or a material conflict remains. Isolated worker failure is evidence, not a reason to erase the wave.

  1. R1ALWAYS

    Discover + propose

    Map the state, collect primary evidence, and generate distinct candidate moves.

  2. R2ALWAYS

    Cross-critique + validate

    Target contradictions, regression risks, weak claims, and missing acceptance evidence.

  3. R3CONDITIONAL

    Corrective frontier

    Run when a survivor, correctable regression, or material testable conflict remains.

  4. R4CONDITIONAL

    Final frontier

    Run only while measurable improvement or a critical resolvable conflict remains.

STOP WHEN

05 · OPERATING INVARIANTS

Force is useful only while it remains governable.

01

One global wave

One 64-task pool per round; never one 64-wide pool per child and never overlapping Sekhmet swarms.

02

Authority stays above

Workers do not coordinate peers, launch swarms, publish, or decide what enters canonical state.

03

Bounded execution

Read-only is the default; mutation candidates receive scope-isolated snapshots.

04

Connectors every round

Cross-axis compatibility is tested structurally, not saved for a final cleanup pass.

05

Evidence-bounded choice

The judge retains only moves whose available evidence improves an axis with no observed regression.

06

One canonical pen

A single writer applies accepted changes sequentially and preserves pre-existing user work.
